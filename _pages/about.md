I am a computational anthropologist who studies the anthropology **_of_** and **_with_** computation. My research examines how cultural meaning is encoded in everything from archaeological archives and global museum collections to digital platforms, marketplaces, and contemporary AI systems. I also build computational methods that enable anthropologists to connect local meaning-making to the large-scale, high-velocity information infrastructures that increasingly mediate cultural life. For me, "scale" is not simply a technical issue but a central site of anthropological inquiry: understanding contemporary culture requires tracing how small, situated practices become entangled with computational systems operating at planetary geographic scope and on ever smaller temporal scales.

I earned my PhD in Anthropology from Harvard University and currently teach Computational Social Science at the University of Chicago.
